# Servo motion-profile bench — measured results

**Run date:** 2026-08-19
**Harness:** `cmd/cli/profile_bench.go` (commit `36fdf39`)
**Raw data:** `bench-out/` (gitignored — 11 CSVs, ~225k sample rows)
**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-servo-motion-profile-bench-design.md`

## Headline

1. **`RegGoalTime` is ignored by this firmware.** Coordinated arrival via the GoalTime
   register — follow-on stage 1 as designed — is not available. A different mechanism is,
   and it is a smaller change than the original plan.
2. **The acceleration register is linear from Acc 1 to ~50, then saturates.** The library's
   "~100 steps/s² per unit" is roughly right in that region. The mapping is per-joint, but
   only by ~1.4×, not the 2–3× that was feared.
3. **feetech's transport flush costs ~26× throughput.** 83 Hz stock vs 2187 Hz with an
   `ResetInputBuffer` flush. Fixing it upstream is a one-line change and is the single
   highest-value action arising from this run.

**The stock rows are flat, as predicted.** ~12.5 ms per transaction regardless of gap,
confirming `SerialTransport.Flush`'s blocking 10 ms read dominates everything. Measured
83 Hz against a predicted ~95 Hz — the flush is ~12.5 ms in practice, not 10 ms.

**The 500 µs row is the sweet spot, and the fastest rows are a trap.** At `none` and
250 µs the harness reports 2187/2239 Hz, but both exceed the 2083 Hz physical wire ceiling
(48 bytes at 1 Mbaud = 480 µs), which means writes are returning before the bytes are on
the wire. The cost shows up in the tail: **p99 of 12.2 ms and a max of 19.8 ms**, i.e. the
USB buffer fills and then stalls. At 500 µs the distribution collapses to
p50 573 µs / p99 596 µs / max 597 µs — a 24 µs spread across 500 writes.

For anything timed, **500 µs gap is the operating point**: ~1750 Hz with essentially no
jitter. Pushing harder buys ~25% throughput and costs a 20 ms tail.

## 2. GoalTime is not honored (`goaltime_servo1_*`, `goaltime_fullarm_*`)

True travel time, re-derived from the raw traces (see *Measurement caveat* below):

| travel | cmd 200 ms | 400 ms | 800 ms | 1600 ms | 3000 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10° | 335 ms | 347 ms | 343 ms | 330 ms | 332 ms |
| 20° | 541 ms | 494 ms | 549 ms | 540 ms | 540 ms |
| 40° | 774 ms | 783 ms | 785 ms | 789 ms | 783 ms |

**Travel time depends only on distance. Commanded time has no effect whatsoever** — a 10°
move takes ~335 ms whether you ask for 200 ms or 3000 ms, a 15× range.

The motion is a clean acceleration-limited triangular profile. Predicting travel time from
the *independent* acceleration sweep, `t = 2·√(d/a)` with `a = 3090 steps/s²` (servo 1 at
the `Acc: 20` this test used):

| travel | predicted | measured | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10° | 384 ms | 335 ms | +14.6% |
| 20° | 543 ms | 540 ms | +0.5% |
| 40° | 768 ms | 783 ms | −2.0% |

Two datasets collected minutes apart, agreeing within 2% on the 20° and 40° cases. The
servo obeys `Acc` and ignores `Time`.

### Arrival spread (`-full-arm`)

Five joints commanded different travels under the same commanded time. If GoalTime worked,
spread would be ~0.

| commanded | 10° block | 20° block | 40° block |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 ms | 267 ms | 343 ms | 519 ms |
| 400 ms | 268 ms | 344 ms | 521 ms |
| 800 ms | 263 ms | 323 ms | 517 ms |
| 1600 ms | 275 ms | 352 ms | 508 ms |
| 3000 ms | 259 ms | 354 ms | 514 ms |

Spread is **260–520 ms and completely independent of commanded time**, varying only with
travel. Joints arrive strictly in order of distance (t40deg_800ms: J1 7.8° at 0.385 s →
J5 39.8° at 0.902 s). This is precisely the independent-joint behaviour the module already
has; the GoalTime register changed nothing.

Implied acceleration, steps/s²:

| Acc | servo 1 | servo 2 | servo 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 627 | 237 | 206 |
| 2 | 717 | 406 | 371 |
| 5 | 1254 | 745 | 884 |
| 10 | 1838 | 1143 | 1684 |
| 20 | 3090 | 2725 | 2528 |

| 200 | 6822 | 5314 | 7216 |
| 255 | 7533 | 5313 | 5336 |
| 0 (unlimited) | 6886 | 4528 | 7993 |

Least-squares over the linear region (Acc 1–50):

| servo | slope | offset | ceiling | knee | ceiling in deg/s² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (base, unloaded) | **127.6** /unit | +533 | 7099 | Acc ≈ 52 | 624 |
| 2 (shoulder, loaded) | **89.3** /unit | +329 | 5282 | Acc ≈ 55 | 464 |
| 3 (elbow, loaded) | **108.2** /unit | +296 | 7140 | Acc ≈ 63 | 628 |

- **The library's ~100 steps/s²/unit is vindicated** — measured 89–128 across three joints.
- **Acc above ~50 does nothing.** Values 50–255 all land within noise of the ceiling, and
  `Acc: 0` (documented as an unlimited ramp) matches it. Usable range is **1–50**.
- **The mapping is per-joint, but mildly.** 89 vs 128 steps/s²/unit is a 1.4× spread — a
  single global constant would be wrong by up to ~30%, which is tolerable for a config
  attribute but worth a per-joint table if precision matters.
- **The config's 500 deg/s² ceiling is unreachable on servo 2** (464 deg/s²). The validated
  range of 10–500 should be clamped per joint, or the upper bound lowered.

## Measurement caveat (a harness bug, found during analysis)

The `measured_ms` column in the goaltime summaries is **unreliable and should not be used**.
`analyzeMove` treats any non-zero velocity as motion, but the servo **dithers ±3 steps
(~0.26°) around its setpoint after arriving**, at ±50–100 steps/s. For short moves that
dither is most of the reported duration: the 10°/3000 ms trial reports 877 ms of which only
~332 ms is travel.

The numbers in §2 are re-derived from the raw sample CSVs using an arrival criterion
(first sample within 4 steps of the settled final position) instead of a velocity
criterion. Keeping per-sample CSVs is what made that recovery possible.

The dither is real servo behaviour, not a sampling artifact, and is worth knowing
independently: **expect ~0.26° of steady-state hunting at rest.**

## What this means for the three follow-on stages

### Stage 1 — coordinated arrival: **viable, but not via GoalTime**

The planned mechanism does not work. The alternative is smaller:

`SafeSoArmController.writePositions` (`manager.go:36`) already calls
`SetPositionsWithSpeed(ctx, rawPositions, speeds)` with a **per-servo** speed map — it just
fills every entry with the same value. Setting `speed_i ∝ d_i` so every joint finishes
together is a handful of lines in a function that already has the right shape.

Caveat from this data: speed-based coordination assumes the profile is speed-limited, not
acceleration-limited. At `Acc: 20` a 228-step move ramps in ~57 ms over ~11 steps, so the
trapezoid is dominated by its cruise phase and the approximation holds. For short moves or
low `Acc` it breaks down, and per-joint `Acc` would need solving alongside speed.

### Stage 2 — acceleration enforcement: **fully specified by this run**

`acceleration_degs_per_sec_per_sec` → `Acc` is `Acc = (deg_s2 × 11.378 − offset) / slope`,
clamped to 1–50, with per-joint slope/offset from the table above. The gap documented in
`README.md:104` can be closed with a lookup and a clamp.

### Stage 3 — trajectory execution: **gated on the feetech flush**

- With the stock transport: 83 Hz. 100 Hz setpoint streaming is **not achievable**.
- With `ResetInputBuffer`: ~1750 Hz at 500 µs gap, p99 within 24 µs of p50. 100 Hz
  streaming has ~17× headroom.

**Recommended next action, independent of all of the above:** change
`SerialTransport.Flush()` in `hipsterbrown/feetech-servo` to use `ResetInputBuffer()`
instead of a read-and-discard loop. One line, ~26× throughput, and it benefits every
consumer of the library rather than just this module.

## Run notes

- All motion tests ran on the fast-flush transport. No stock-transport motion runs were
  taken; `writerate` covers the transport comparison.
- No trial hit `sampling_timed_out`, and no trial reported `ramp_below_sampling_resolution`
  — sample counts were 1600–6600 per trial, ample throughout.
- Overshoot was 0–1 steps everywhere. The servos do not overshoot meaningfully at any
  tested `Acc`.
- `verifyAtStart` did not abort any sweep, so the return legs were all clean.
